I2C · Wii extensions

The KB2040's I2C bus on its STEMMA QT port, and any Wii extension controller on the adapter at the far end of the cable, found, identified and read by NintendoExtensionCtrl every /rate tick, and drawn above as it moves. Every kind the library reads is on the shelf below, labelled with the library class that reads it.

Wiringone STEMMA QT cable · GPIO12 SDA · GPIO13 SCL · 3.3 V

One STEMMA QT (Qwiic) cable, a four-pin JST SH plug at each end, from the KB2040's STEMMA QT port to either of the two on Adafruit's Wii Nunchuck Breakout Adapter, and the extension's plug onto the adapter's tongue, its notched side of the connector U goes on the side that says Notch Up. Nothing else: no breadboard, no jumpers, nothing on the adapter's header.

Both boards' schematics put GND, the supply, SDA and SCL on their connectors' pads 1 to 4, and the cable joins each pad to the same one at the far end. The KB2040's supply pad is its 3.3 V and the adapter's is its VIN, so the adapter runs from the KB2040's 3.3 V, as its guide asks of VIN: give it the same power as the logic level of your microcontroller. SDA is GPIO12 and SCL GPIO13, the RP2040's I2C0 (the KB2040's guide and its schematic), which arduino-pico's adafruit_kb2040 variant gives Wire. Drawn face up, the two sockets hold their pads in mirror order along the cable, so the cable turns over once between its plugs.

Pull-ups. The KB2040 pulls SDA and SCL up to its 3.3 V through 5.1K (R10 and R12), always, and the adapter pulls its microcontroller side of each up to VIN through 10K (its extension side, past its level shifter, to its own 3.3 V through 10K). So there are no pull-up lamps here: XiaoSamd21Oscuino reads each line against the chip's pull-down to find a pull-up outside the board, and on this board that reading could not tell whether the adapter is there at all. Before the bus starts the board reads each line as a plain input, and a line held LOW against those pull-ups stops the bus starting; /diag says so.

How an extension is foundNintendoExtensionCtrl 0.8.3 · 100 kHz

While the stream runs and nothing is plugged in, the board tries the bus on the first tick 250 ms or more after its last try: it calls the library's ExtensionPort::connect(), which initialises whatever answers at 0x52, reads its identity and runs the matching type's own start-up; getControllerType() then names it, and the library's requestIdentity() gives the six bytes shown beside the name. From then on each tick is one update(). When an update fails the board calls connect() again, as the library's MultipleTypes example does; where the example then keeps trying a second apart, the board tries once, and if that fails it forgets the extension and goes back to trying the empty bus. A type the library does not know is shown as Unknown, with the six bytes the library read for it as bits. All of this is XiaoSamd21Oscuino's code, line for line; the sketch has no protocol code of its own.

The bus runs at 100 kHz, arduino-pico's default (TWI_CLOCK), and each call of the library and each probe of the scan is followed by 1 ms of idle bus. On XiaoSamd21Oscuino's SAMD21, with a Nunchuk through this adapter, an identity read straight after connect() failed 30 times of 30, and 1 ms later succeeded 30 times of 30 (that sketch's header has the runs). Neither the gap nor the clock has been measured on a KB2040.

The scan, /i2c/scan, is not the library's: a one-byte read of each address from 0x08 to 0x77, 1 ms apart, since a read writes nothing to whatever is there.

The fields/wii, in this order

Each field is one of the library's getters, named as the library names it, in the order its header declares them: every public, const getter that takes no argument and returns a number or a boolean, of the type's own class, and for the turntable then those of its left and right platters, from the class both are made from. Left out are getExpectedType(), which every class declares and which returns the library's ExtensionType, and the getters every class inherits from ExtensionController, such as getControllerType(). Ranges are the library's comments on those getters, or the getter's type where it gives none; buttons are the library's booleans, 1 while pressed. The turntable's differ from its comment: by the library's code each platter's turntable() is five bits and a sign, -32..31, and the combined one their sum, where the comment on it says 6 bits, -30-29. A platter's connected reads 1 only while it turns or one of its buttons is held: the library remembers a platter only in getTurntableConfig(), which /wii does not call (its DJTurntable.cpp; no turntable was tried here).
