XIAO SAMD21 Touch

Three touch libraries on one XIAO SAMD21, one at a time, chosen live: Adafruit FreeTouch, FastTouch and CapacitiveSensor. Each streams /cap, with its set-up under /cap/<param>. Chrome or Edge, over Web Serial.
not connected
–

FastTouch has no settings. Each read drives the pin LOW for 1 ms, releases it to its internal pull-up and counts how many of 23 back-to-back reads still see it LOW, so a scan of all eleven pins takes at least 11 ms.

No pins until you choose them: each receive pin needs a resistor to the send pin (see below). Without one every read times out and reads −2.

Values in orange differ from the sketch's defaults.

FreeTouch: what the set-up parameters do

FreeTouch measures self-capacitance: each pad is one PTC Y line, measured on its own. The register names are FreeTouch's. The Microchip names beside them are matched by name and value range from Microchip's own touch libraries, listed at the end.

oversample1 2 4 8 16 32 64 samples · default 4

How many conversions the PTC sums into one reading: FreeTouch's oversample, written to CONVCTRL.ADCACCUM. QTouch 5.0.1 calls it the filter level (FILTER_LEVEL_1 to FILTER_LEVEL_64); the QTouch Modular Library calls it the Accumulator setting. The sketch divides the sum by the count, as FreeTouch's measure() does, so /cap stays on the same scale at every setting.

Measured here: one scan of all seven pads took 249 µs at 1, 413 µs at 4, 1038 µs at 16 and 3556 µs at 64. Resting readings stayed within 3 counts across the four settings. Its effect on noise was not established by these runs.

resistor0 20 50 100 kΩ · default 50

An internal resistor in series with the pad: FreeTouch's seriesres, written to SERRES.RESISTOR; QTouch's RSEL_VAL_0 to RSEL_VAL_100. Microchip's sources disagree about self-capacitance mode, which is the mode FreeTouch uses. QTouch 5.0.1: For Self cap mode, thes series resistor is switched internally on the Sensor pin. The QTouch Modular SAMD21 header: PTC series resistor setting, only works in mutual capacitance mode. The Modular Library guide's known issues: When PTC is used in Self-Capacitance mode, the internal series resistor is not effective in reducing noise.

Measured here: against 50 kΩ, 100 kΩ lowered D0 by 28 counts and D1 by 5, and raised the other five pads by 2 to 4. At 0, 20 and 50 kΩ every pad read within 4 counts of itself. So it is in the circuit on this board; whether it reduces noise was not measured.

freq0 none · 1 hop · 2 spread · 3 spread-median · default 0

Noise countermeasures, named after QTouch 5.0.1's frequency modes: No Frequency hopping, Frequency hopping is enabled, spread spectrum with median filter disabled and with median filter enabled. The Modular Library describes spread as one that automatically sweeps through all 15 delay values during the series of measurements. FreeTouch 1.1.3 does not implement four modes. Read in its source: for any value other than 0 it sets FREQCTRL.FREQSPREADEN to 1 and SAMPLEDELAY to hop − 1, so 1, 2 and 3 behave the same here. Which of QTouch's behaviours that produces was not measured.

Measured here: at rest, 1 against 0 changed no pad by more than 2 counts. It was not tried against a noise source, which is what it is for.

hop1..16 · default 1

The acquisition frequency delay: FreeTouch's hops, written to FREQCTRL.SAMPLEDELAY as hop − 1, and only when freq is not 0. QTouch 5.0.1: the setting inserts "n" PTC clock cycles between consecutive measurements on a given sensor, thereby changing the PTC acquisition frequency, so a higher delay setting will increase the total time taken for capacitance measurement. And: A desired setting can be used to avoid noise around the same frequency as the acquisition frequency.

Measured here: only that it is echoed. Its effect on readings and on scan time was not measured.

compcap0..16383 · default 8192 (0x2000)

The compensation capacitor code: FreeTouch's compcap, 14 bits written to COMPCAPH:COMPCAPL. Microchip's libraries set this for each sensor by calibration. The Modular Library keeps a per-node node_comp_caps, found in a CC Calibration state that flags CAL ERROR - Max comp cap when it runs out of range. FreeTouch instead starts every pad at the same 0x2000. No source opened here gives its value in picofarads.

Measured here: 4000 in place of 8192 raised every pad by 82 to 87 counts. A lower value gave a higher count on every pad.

intcap0..63 · default 63

FreeTouch's name for a 6-bit register (INTCAP, its "Int Cap reg"), which FreeTouch sets to 63 and never changes. None of the Microchip sources opened here names it. Their APIs expose an analog gain per sensor instead (QTouch 5.0.1 GAIN_1 to GAIN_32; the Modular Library GAIN_1 to GAIN_16, packed with a digital gain), and neither says which register implements it. Treat "integration capacitor" as FreeTouch's label, unconfirmed.

Measured here: 10 and 20 on one pad each drove its reading to 0, and the other pads did not move. The default 63 gives the resting counts on the panel above.

Not settable here

The PTC clock prescaler (QTouch PRSC_DIV_SEL_1 to PRSC_DIV_SEL_8, which sets PTC Clock to 4MHz down to 500 kHz from a 4 MHz input). Read in FreeTouch's source: begin() clears bits in the register its debug printer labels "Prescaler" (0x42004C04) and nothing writes it again.

FastTouch: how it measures

Adrian Freed's library, version 0.91, MIT licence. It needs nothing but the pin, so it measures all eleven header pins, D2 to D5 included.

What a reading is0..23 on SAMD21 · no settings

Its README: it returns a measure of how long it takes for a pin to rise to a HIGH level after the pull up is enabled. This will be slowed by any capacitive load added. And: 0 = no touch, higher = more capacitance (touch detected). The 2018 article that introduced it: The sensed value is reported as a function of the time it takes for the pull-up to bring the pin to a high value. and each call to the fast touch library implements a cycle of a relaxation oscillator.

Read in the 0.91 source, SAMD21 branch: fastTouchRead(pin) is a macro that sets the pin to OUTPUT LOW, waits delay(1), switches it to INPUT_PULLUP, reads the port's IN register 23 times back to back, and counts the reads that still see the pin LOW. So it reads 0 to 23, which is what fastTouchMax() returns on SAMD21. Two things to know. The article gives the discharge as set the pin to output a LOW value for 1uS or so., but this branch waits 1 ms, which sets the scan time here (at least 11 ms for eleven pins). And the macro's /* disable interrupts */ and /* enable interrupts */ comments mark places where nothing is called, so an interrupt during the 23 reads would stretch them; whether that happens here was not measured.

Measured here: untouched, all eleven pins read 0. With the PTC still enabled from FreeTouch, as the sketch first left it, one recorded touch (2026-10-28) raised D2..D5, the four pins with no Y line, by 4 to 7 counts, and the seven Y-line pins stayed at 0. The sketch now turns the PTC and its Y lines off before FastTouch takes the pins, and /diag reads them back (PTC off, Y 0x0000). After that change, touched by hand through this page, every one of the eleven pins rose; that was observed, not recorded.

CapacitiveSensor: how it measures, and how to wire it

Paul Badger's library, maintained by Paul Stoffregen, version 0.5.1; its source carries an MIT-style permission notice. Its library.properties: it turns two or more Arduino pins into a capacitive sensor, which can sense the electrical capacitance of the human body. All the sensor setup requires is a medium to high value resistor and a piece of wire and a small (to large) piece of aluminum foil on the end.

Wiringa send pin, and a resistor to each receive pin

PJRC's page: Each sensor connects to 2 pins, send and receive. The send pin must connect with a large-value resistor, between 100K to 50M. The receive pin may be connected with a wire, but a 1K or higher resistor will help protect the Teensy's pin. Multiple sensors can share a single send pin, but each must have its own receive pin. On the resistor: Larger values allow more sensitivity, but with slower response. The library's own example gives a wider range, Resistor effects sensitivity, experiment with values, 50K - 50M. Larger resistor values yield larger sensor values., and uses a high value resistor e.g. 10M between send pin and receive pin.

What a reading isa count, or −2 for a timeout

PJRC: The elapsed time, in arbitrary units, is returned. and A negative number is returned if any error occurs. Read in the source: each charge cycle drives the send pin HIGH and counts loop passes until the receive pin reads HIGH, then charges it and drives the send pin LOW and counts until it reads LOW; capacitiveSensorRaw(samples), which this sketch calls, returns the total over samples cycles with no baseline subtracted, or −2 once the total passes the timeout. The sketch passes −2 through unchanged.

samples1..255 · default 30

PJRC: The measurement is repeated numSamples times. More measurements increases sensitivity, but takes longer. 30 is what the library's example passes.

timeout1..60000 · default 2000

Passed to set_CS_Timeout_Millis(). Read in the source: the library turns it into a loop count, timeout × loopTimingFactor × F_CPU / 16000000, with a loopTimingFactor = 310 its comment calls determined empirically - a hack, so it is milliseconds only nominally. 2000 is its constructor's value.

Measured here, with no resistors fitted, so every read timed out (−2): at 2000 a scan of two pads took 1.40 s, about 0.70 s per pad; at 20 the same scan took 14.1 ms. The board reads one pad per pass and answers OSC between pads, so a message waits for at most the pad being read: with four pads timing out (scans of 2.65 s), echoes came back in 0.16 to 0.59 s (2026-10-29). The sensor itself has not been measured: this board has no resistors fitted.

I2C · Wii extensions

The board's I2C bus on D4 and D5, and any Wii extension controller plugged into it, found, identified and read by NintendoExtensionCtrl every /rate tick, and drawn above as it moves. Every kind the library reads is on the shelf below, labelled with the library class that reads it.

WiringD4 SDA · D5 SCL · 3V3 · GND

An extension's plug slides onto Adafruit's Wii Nunchuck Breakout Adapter, whose header pins are VIN, 3V, GND, SCL and SDA. Its guide: VIN is the power pin, give it the same power as the logic level of your microcontroller, so the XIAO's 3V3 goes to VIN, not to 3V, which is the 3.3V output from the voltage regulator; SCL and SDA are level shifted, each with a 10K pull-up. The adapter's schematic pulls the microcontroller's side of both up to VIN, which is why VIN is not the XIAO's 5V: Seeed's wiki says of its pins Voltage input connected to general I/O pins may cause chip damage if it' higher than 3.3V. SCL goes to D5 and SDA to D4, the XIAO variant's Wire pins (PIN_WIRE_SCL 5, PIN_WIRE_SDA 4, on SERCOM2). While the bus is on they are not touch pads: the board measures no pads, and a touch tab gives them back.

Pull-ups. Before Wire takes the pins the board reads each line with the SAMD21's own pull-down on, so a lamp lit means something outside held the line up, as the adapter's 10K do. Then it turns its own pull-ups on, which Wire leaves set, and a line that is still LOW stops the bus starting at all, because the core's Wire waits on the bus with no timeout. How strong the internal pull-ups are was not looked up or measured here.

How an extension is foundNintendoExtensionCtrl 0.8.3 · 100 kHz

Every 250 ms while nothing is plugged in, the board calls the library's ExtensionPort::connect(), which initialises whatever answers at 0x52, reads its identity and runs the matching type's own start-up; getControllerType() then names it, and the library's requestIdentity() gives the six bytes shown beside the name. From then on each tick is one update(). When an update fails the board calls connect() again, as the library's MultipleTypes example does; where the example then keeps trying a second apart, the board tries once, and if that fails it forgets the extension and asks the empty bus every 250 ms. A type the library does not know is shown as Unknown, with the six bytes the library read for it as bits. The sketch has no protocol code of its own.

Measured on this board with a Nunchuk through the adapter, VIN on 3V3, 2026-10-29, by the library alone (test/hardware/WiiLibProbe, its output in wiilibprobe-2026-10-29.log beside it): at 100 kHz, the core's default, update() succeeded 90 times of 90 a second, over 8 s; at 400 kHz, over the seven whole seconds after the switch, 1..9 times of 19..26. The bus stays at 100 kHz.

The scan, /i2c/scan, is not the library's: a one-byte read of each address from 0x08 to 0x77, 1 ms apart, since a read writes nothing to whatever is there. In four runs of the probe at 100 kHz, a scan that went from address to address with no gap found nothing, by empty write (what most scanners send) or by one-byte read; probed alone in ten rounds, each probe 1 ms after the last, the Nunchuk answered 9 empty writes, 10 one-byte reads and 10 pointer writes of 10, every run. The first empty write of each run followed the scan with no gap; whether it was the one missed was not separated. In two runs at 400 kHz the scan with no gap found nothing in one and 0x52 both ways in the other, and alone the Nunchuk answered 4 and 5 empty writes, 5 and 5 one-byte reads and 3 and 3 pointer writes of 10.

The fields/wii, in this order

Each field is one of the library's getters, named as the library names it, in the order its header declares them: every public, const getter that takes no argument and returns a number or a boolean, of the type's own class, and for the turntable then those of its left and right platters, from the class both are made from. Left out are getExpectedType(), which every class declares and which returns the library's ExtensionType, and the getters every class inherits from ExtensionController, such as getControllerType(). Ranges are the library's comments on those getters, or the getter's type where it gives none; buttons are the library's booleans, 1 while pressed. The turntable's differ from its comment: by the library's code each platter's turntable() is five bits and a sign, -32..31, and the combined one their sum, where the comment on it says 6 bits, -30-29. A platter's connected reads 1 only while it turns or one of its buttons is held: the library remembers a platter only in getTurntableConfig(), which /wii does not call (its DJTurntable.cpp; no turntable was tried here).
